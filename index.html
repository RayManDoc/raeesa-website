Lecturer in Statistics at the University of the Witwatersrand, specialising in applied & spatial statistics, stochastic modelling, and survival analysis.

Dr. Manjoo-Docrat develops computational methods for understanding infectious disease dynamics, with a focus on spatio-stochastic models that capture the spatially heterogeneous nature of epidemic spread. She completed a post-doctoral fellowship at Johns Hopkins University focused on modelling tuberculosis transmission.

She is a founding member of SEPIMOD — the Spatial Epidemic Modelling Group — an interdisciplinary consortium advancing public health research across South Africa.

01
Spatio-Stochastic Modelling of Infectious Disease Spread

Developing spatial components integrated into discrete-time binomial chain models for nuanced understanding of disease transmission dynamics, including local transmission, inter-regional interactions, and migration between spatial units.

02
Spatial Epidemiology of COVID-19

Spatially explicit SEIR models incorporating a vulnerability index reflecting socioeconomic and health-related disparities, alongside mobility data, illustrated using South African COVID-19 data to inform targeted public health responses.

03
Wastewater-Based Epidemiology (WBE)

Exploring cost-effective community-level disease surveillance through wastewater analysis, developing spatial methods for early-warning systems and hotspot identification in urban and peri-urban settings.

04
Modelling Tuberculosis in Vulnerable Populations

Individual-based models for TB prevalence in high-risk populations, incorporating COVID-19 disruptions and developing spatial optimisation tools for vaccination and resource allocation.

05
Innovative Approaches to Teaching R in Statistics

Examining R programming integration into undergraduate and postgraduate curricula, evaluating pedagogical strategies, and developing open-source R packages for spatial epidemiological modelling.
